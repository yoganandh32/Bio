01 / ABOUT

An engineer’s mindset.
A researcher’s curiosity.

I’m Yoganandh, an acoustics researcher and NVH engineer working where waves, materials, and mechanical systems meet.

My PhD at Michigan State University explored nonlinear acoustic wave propagation in inhomogeneous media. I developed theoretical and experimental approaches to reveal material behavior through ultrasound—from layered media to internal stress and fatigue cracks.

Today, at W.M. Associates, I bring that same approach to noise, vibration, and harshness: combining testing, signal processing, and modeling to turn complex measurements into useful engineering insights.

I also enjoy mentoring and sharing the tools of research. At MSU, I taught control and manufacturing laboratories and mentored graduate students in wave modeling and ultrasonic evaluation.

06 / MASTER’S RESEARCH · IIT HYDERABAD

Predicting noise
through energy flow.

My master’s research used Statistical Energy Analysis (SEA) to predict breakout noise from rectangular HVAC ducts. I compared SEA predictions with finite and boundary element methods in COMSOL and investigated the insertion loss provided by acoustic lagging.

SEA models how energy is exchanged between coupled acoustic and structural subsystems. This gives a way to study the connection between sound inside a duct, vibration of its walls, and sound radiated outside.

  • Statistical Energy Analysis
  • Duct acoustics
  • FEM / BEM
  • Noise control
